Before there is a database, a telecentre keeps a spreadsheet. One row per
learner: the name, the e-mail, the telecentre, its neighbourhood and city, and a
last cell with the assessments taken, separated by semicolons. The lab builds
that spreadsheet from the very rows the OpenLabs database holds, so that every
problem below is counted, not described.

## Three ways a spreadsheet lies

Normalization theory names three anomalies, each a way for the data to
contradict itself or lose something ([Codd 1971](#src-codd-1971)). In the
spreadsheet they are easy to see.

- **Update.** "Telecentro Jardim Ângela" is typed on four rows. Renaming it means
  changing four cells. Change three, and the spreadsheet holds two names for
  the same place, with nothing to say which is right.
- **Insertion.** A new telecentre opens before its first learner. There is no
  row to put it in, because every row is a learner.
- **Deletion.** Remove the only attempt at the Splunk checkpoint, and the lab's
  title and the handbook's name disappear from the data. They were only ever
  written next to that attempt.

None of these is about disk space. Each one leads to a wrong answer.

## First normal form: one value per cell

The last cell holds a list. A list is a value the database cannot check, join or
count, and removing columns whose values are themselves sets is the first step
Codd describes ([Codd 1970](#src-codd-1970)). Unpack it into one row per
attempt, and the key becomes `(id_aluno, id_avaliacao, dt_envio)`.

The cost is immediate, and the lab measures it. Learners who never took an
assessment have no attempt, so they have no row: 7 of 12 learners remain. The
telecentre's name is now typed five times instead of four, once per attempt
of its learners. First normal form makes the data regular without yet making
it right.

## Second normal form: the whole key

`nm_aluno` does not depend on the whole key. It depends on `id_aluno` alone, and
the assessment's name, its lab and its handbook depend on `id_avaliacao` alone.
Second normal form moves each group to a table keyed by what it depends on:
`ALUNO_2FN`, `AVALIACAO_2FN`, and the attempts themselves, which keep only what
belongs to an attempt, the date and the grade
([Codd 1971](#src-codd-1971)).

Two anomalies go away. All twelve learners have a row again, and deleting
attempts no longer erases the catalogue. One remains: `ALUNO_2FN` still carries
the telecentre's neighbourhood and city on every learner.

## Third normal form: nothing but the key

`nm_bairro` is a fact about the telecentre, and the telecentre is a fact about
the learner. A fact about a fact is a transitive dependency, and third normal
form removes it. Kent's sentence is the one to remember: every non-key column
must provide a fact about the key, the whole key, and nothing but the key
([Kent](#src-kent-1983)). The telecentre moves to `T_TELECENTRO`, and the learner
keeps only `id_telecentro`, a foreign key. The same chain on the catalogue side
(assessment, lab, phase, handbook) becomes `T_AVALIACAO`, `T_LAB`, `T_FASE` and
`T_HANDBOOK`.

These are seven of the ten tables in `01-ddl.sql`, with the same rows. Renaming
the telecentre is now one cell. A telecentre without learners is one row in
`T_TELECENTRO`. Deleting attempts deletes attempts and nothing else.

| | Spreadsheet | 1NF | 2NF | 3NF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cells to rename one telecentre | 4 | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| learners the tables can hold | 12 | 7 | 12 | 12 |
| a telecentre with no learners | no | no | no | yes |
| names lost deleting one attempt | 3 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

## What the normal forms do not decide

Normalization tells you where each fact goes. It does not choose names, types or
rules, and it does not say whether the key should be the natural one. The final
schema gives every table a surrogate key, an `IDENTITY` column the database
fills by itself ([CREATE TABLE](#src-oracle-create-table)), and keeps the
natural facts (learner, assessment, date) as ordinary columns protected by
foreign keys.

> [!deeper] Why give T_TENTATIVA its own id when (id_aluno, id_avaliacao, dt_envio) already identifies an attempt?
>
> In 2NF the natural key was enough. A surrogate key makes everything that
> refers to the row simpler: one column instead of three in a URL such as
> `/tentativas/9`, in a foreign key, in a `WHERE`. It also survives a change in
> the rules, for example two attempts on the same day. With
> `GENERATED BY DEFAULT ON NULL AS IDENTITY`, an insert that leaves the id out,
> as the Java API's POST does, gets the next number from the database, while
> the sample data can still insert fixed ids ([CREATE TABLE](#src-oracle-create-table)).
> If the natural combination must still be unique, a `UNIQUE` constraint says
> so, as `UNIQUE (id_aluno, id_lab)` does in `T_PROGRESSO`.
